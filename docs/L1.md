# L1 — Evaluating a rational Bézier map

The increment started at commit `da78c69`. The initial `make check` passed with three tests and six upstream references. The assigned roles were implementation in `trivariate/spline.py`, independent oracles under `tests/`, and CLI integration. The working branch was `l1-rational-bezier`.

## Contract and criteria fixed at the start

- Tensor-product map on the closed cube `[0,1]^3`, with degrees `(1,1,1)` and `(2,1,1)`. Homogeneous coefficients `(wx,wy,wz,w)` must be finite and weights strictly positive. The k index varies fastest. Physical coordinates are in mm. On a face, fix the constant parameter and preserve the relative order of the other two axes.
- Evaluation returns `X` and the three columns of `DX` in the order `∂uX`, `∂vX`, `∂wX`. Parameters are dimensionless. The contract includes boundaries, edges, and vertices.
- Check coordinates and every component of `DX` to an absolute error of `10⁻¹² mm` on the fixed recipes. Use an independent analytical expression for the quarter annulus. The numerical recipe stores the weight `√2/2` in double precision.
- Examine malformed inputs, zero weights, unsupported degrees, and parameters outside the cube. Replay historical tests and verify the CLI path.
- This increment qualifies evaluation on the tested recipes. Adopting a complete curved solid with regularity, injectivity, and partition proofs belongs to the next milestone.

## Results

With Python 3.12.3, `make check` checks the six references, runs the unit suite, and replays the historical affine model. On the 125-point grid of the quarter annulus (five values per axis, including boundaries), the largest observed absolute errors are `4.44 × 10⁻¹⁶ mm` for `X` and `8.88 × 10⁻¹⁶ mm` for components of `DX`. The oracle uses the rational arc expression and its analytical derivatives.

The asymmetric-weight test checks all three denominator derivatives against a fraction-based calculation at the center of a quadratic map. Integration found and fixed an error in the quadratic basis derivative and a missing term in the denominator derivative of the quarter-annulus oracle. The initial thresholds were retained.

`python3 -m trivariate evaluate tests/references/quarter_annulus.json 0.5 0.5 0.5` returns `(1.0606601718, 1.0606601718, 1.5) mm` and the three columns of `DX`. The next qualification concerns the shared interface between two curved blocks.

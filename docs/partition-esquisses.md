# From Sketch Construction Lines to Trivariate Blocks

## Research question

Given a feature history, solved sketches, axes, and material operations, generate partition curves, the interior surfaces they induce, and the associated volumetric maps. The user supplies the design features. The block plan is an inspectable output that can be reused after edits. The existing elasticity calculation serves as a downstream test.

## Proposed rules for the Rust compiler

These rules are implementation choices and test criteria for SolidCraftTrivariate. Guarantees apply to qualified families.

1. **Identify material before tracing.** Build the arrangement of segments and arcs, split at recognized intersections, identify simple loops, their containment, and the faces selected by the feature. For explicit even–odd filling, successive levels alternate material, void, and island. For additive or subtractive operations, apply the operation semantics to the current solid. Two disjoint loops can represent two parts, two holes, or selectable regions. An ordinary CAD construction line remains a reference; using it as a cut requires an identifiable decision.
2. **Build a conforming planar graph.** Candidate lines attach at reflex corners, primitive changes, fillet endpoints, circle sectors, or imprints of the next feature. Each accepted intersection becomes a shared vertex. Preserve each edge's support, parameter interval, provenance, and role as a boundary or interior separator. Final material cells have four logical sides and disk topology. A separator connecting a hole to another contour can open the domain for parameterization; the two physical sides of the cut must then be identified correctly.
3. **Count the subdivision actually produced.** For a regular planar domain with c material components and h holes, verify V − E + Q = c − h and 4Q = 2 E_int + E_bord for a fully quadrilateral conforming decomposition. Thus E_bord is even on each component, counting all its contours together. An annulus can have two contours with odd edge counts. A common subdivision can restore parity. These identities rule out impossible solutions; they do not select suitable cuts or prove an injective map.
4. **Choose among admissible partitions.** For an isolated circular hole, try a ring of sectors between exact arcs and a guard contour. A radial sector cannot close at zero radius in a regular block. To fill a disk, provide a positive-area core and peripheral blocks. For several holes, build a visible, noncrossing connection graph that respects ligaments and corners; if two rings interfere, recompute the partition of the shared region. Record rejected candidates. Rank admissible candidates by distortion, complexity, and edit cost with a stable tie-break order.
5. **Split depth at events.** For coaxial or parallel operations, include the start and end of each removal, pocket floor, shoulder, island appearance, and intersection event. Build a common subdivision on both sides of each level. At an interface, material/material requires a shared face, material/void creates an oriented physical boundary, and void/void creates no block. A pocket partly open to the boundary requires reclassifying the contour after the Boolean. For sloped walls or curved sweeps, also detect encounters and order changes between levels.
6. **Change sweep map when axes differ.** A simple extrusion preserves the topological type of a section and the correspondence of its sides. A transverse hole can appear, split a section, and disappear in the first sweep frame. Handle each feature locally in its own frame; seek transition volumes between sweepable regions. Cutting sheets from different axes must form one 3D arrangement. Their intersections are shared edges with explicit endpoint incidences. If the search fails within budget, preserve the proposal and blocking witness for later work.
7. **Check blocks over the continuous domain.** For a straight extrusion X=S(u,v)+w d, the determinant is (S_u × S_v)·d. An injective regular planar section with a transverse direction of suitable sign gives a directly provable case. A loft or change of frame requires checking the whole volume. For each rational block, check the denominator and a strictly positive lower bound for det DX on the closed domain, establish global injectivity, then disjoint interiors and coverage of the expected solid. Compare degrees, knots, weights, and trace coefficients on shared faces after orientation and common refinement. Resolve a T-junction by conforming subdivision or an actually qualified nested interface.

## Guiding example: plate with a blind pocket

Plate 60 × 40 × 20 mm. Remove an interior rectangular pocket 20 × 10 mm from the top, 6 mm deep, with a flat floor. The compiler projects the four pocket sides onto the plate section and adds separators to the outer contour. It preserves the same trace at z=14 on neighboring cells. In the perimeter ring, blocks connect across layers. Beneath the footprint, the lower block ends at the physical pocket-floor face. Oracle volume: 46,800 mm³. At depth 20, the same operation makes a through-hole, with volume 44,000 mm³ and no floor face. An edit crossing this event changes incidences and must update selections.

## Generalization tests to freeze before the campaign

| Independent input | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Same plate, separated holes approaching tangency; renamed, reversed, or cyclically shifted contours | Topology consistent with selection, geometry invariant under alternate encodings, certified ligament before contact, and a defined event or justified refusal at contact. |
| C-shaped contour: (0,0),(20,0),(20,4),(6,4),(6,16),(20,16),(20,20),(0,20). Two square holes of side 2, lower-left corners (12,1) and (15,17). Extrusion height 7 with upper offset (3,−2). | Material area 20² − 14×12 − 2×2² = 224 mm², volume 1,568 mm³. With the reference decomposition and no extra triangulation vertex: 16 boundary vertices, 2 holes, 18 triangles, then 54 blocks. A new planner may use fewer blocks. Also test reversed loops and permuted holes. |
| Pocket, through-hole, and island, with two feature orders whose geometric equivalence is established | Volumes, sections at multiple depths, floor and island faces, material/void classifications inside and outside footprints; geometric equivalence despite distinct history identities. |
| Block 60 × 40 × 30 mm with two radius-3 cylinders along X and Z | First, axes 10 mm apart along Y and fully interior in section: V = 72,000 − 810π mm³. Then perpendicular axes intersecting at the center: V = 72,000 − 810π + 144 mm³ (Steinmetz intersection). Identical local sketches have different 3D consequences. Require the correct boundary and classifications in the shared region as well as volume. Record inability to build this variant as a coverage limit. |
| Target bracket, then unseen dimensions and positions | Start from the feature graph alone, log every choice, and produce the partition without calling the specialized constructor. Replay the geometric oracle, audit traces, and verify reproduction of an affine field. The historical 36-block family is a comparison reference. |

Compare three methods on the same inputs: the current template where applicable, triangulation followed by quadrangulation, and feature-guided separator selection. Measure certified construction rate with a fixed denominator, block count, minimum normalized Jacobian, distortion, certificate cost, region changed after an edit, and DOFs at comparable accuracy. Archive 2D curves, 3D sheets, and refusal reasons. Fewer blocks are an improvement only if map quality and analysis criteria are preserved.

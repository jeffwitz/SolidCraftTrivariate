Crossed-tool partial-interface mechanics

This reference describes the native elasticity check for the ten-chart S2 union of two crossing tools. Here S2 names the crossed-tool geometry experiment in docs/results/crossed-interface-iga; it is unrelated to roadmap stage labels elsewhere in this repository.

The native entry point is crossed_bending::analyze in src/crossed_bending.rs. The filesystem command routes through the same function:

cargo build --release --locked --offline
mkdir -p build
cli=target/release/solidcraft-trivariate
"$cli" crossed-boolean tests/jobs/crossed_partial_rise3.recipe.json union build/inclined-cross.model.json
"$cli" crossed-bending build/inclined-cross.model.json 1 1 8 build/crossed-bending-v1w1q8.json

The stored recipe is tests/jobs/crossed_partial_rise3.recipe.json. It embeds the S2 sketch pair and the positive 3 mm transverse rise. The API replays its embedded source_recipe through g2_cross_plate::boolean::report and requires exact JSON equality with the saved model before accepting its charts. Changing chart identifiers while leaving the source recipe untouched is therefore rejected. The CLI publishes the analysis result atomically only after a successful solve.

Model, field, and loading

The analysis raises the source geometry’s degree-(3,1,1) homogeneous maps to a Q(3,2,2) analysis basis. It subdivides the chart coordinates in v and w; u remains unrefined. The implementation currently accepts 1 <= refine_v <= 4, 1 <= refine_w <= 2 and tensor Gauss orders 4 through 14. The chart geometry remains the replayed source geometry. Positive homogeneous weights are required.

The constitutive model is small-strain, three-dimensional isotropic linear elasticity with Young’s modulus E = 1000 MPa and Poisson ratio nu = 0.3. The left end at x = 0 is clamped. A uniform outward traction of 1 MPa in the positive x direction is integrated over the right end; all other boundaries are traction-free. The reported displacement is the traction-weighted mean x displacement on that loaded end. Thus its work conjugate is the reported total x force, 40.2222222222 N for the checked geometry.

Interior chart interfaces and within-chart subdivisions identify their full Q(3,2,2) coefficient traces. The four wing-to-core interfaces are partial: the wing and core parameterizations have different interface coverage. The current route constrains these traces by overdetermined, preimage-aware collocation and then computes a numerical nullspace of the resulting trace matrix. It samples 21 points in u and 4 in each slave w cell. This is not an exact mortar method, a proof of trace compatibility, or a general nonmatching-interface algorithm. Its mapping assumptions are specific to the recorded ten-chart S2 geometry, including the physical height relation used to locate the master w cell. To resolve child-knot crossings, the native path samples 65 points along each relevant edge, bisects sign-changing brackets, then uses 21 points in each resulting u segment. At each u it splits the slave w interval at uniform child knots and at physical z = 12 k / refine_w crossings, and places four points on every resulting subinterval.

The native test covers v=1,w=1,q8; v=4,w=1,q8/q14; and the two published w=2 rows. It also checks a real CLI invocation and that malformed geometry leaves an existing output file byte-for-byte intact. The dedicated CLI parity script replays all 13 published rows, including zero and negative transverse rise. Run the bounded native plus independent Python gate from the root:

cargo test --locked --offline --release --test crossed_bending
make check-crossed-bending

Scope and failure interpretation

The trace rank is determined numerically with relative cutoff 1e-12. The native result reports that rank, the maximum sampled geometric gap, an affine patch energy error, a projected equilibrium residual, and a stiffness symmetry diagnostic. These are checks on this particular discretization and run, not certificates of injectivity, global trace equality, mesh convergence, or solution error. The Python study additionally reports held-out trace samples, six rigid modes and an off-collocation displacement jump; those independent diagnostics are not yet reproduced by the native API. A matrix-free symmetry check uses six prescribed bilinear probes; it is not an exhaustive matrix symmetry test.

The native route does not currently solve a manufactured-solution problem (MMS) with a prescribed exact field and matching body force. Its affine energy patch check and agreement with the independent S2 model are complementary diagnostics, not an MMS substitute. They do not establish general-order convergence for arbitrary loads or geometries.

Native parity is measured for rises -3, 0, +3 mm at (v,w) refinements (1,1), (2,1), and (4,1); positive-rise cases additionally cover (1,2) and (2,2). The finest supported inclined row for w=2 in the published study is v=2; no v=4,w=2 oracle row exists. Nonlinear material response, contact, and arbitrary partial interfaces remain unqualified. Singular fillets are outside this smooth crossed-tool mechanics case; their stress convergence needs separate local refinement and an independent reference.

Method context

The shared spline basis and geometry refinement follow the general isogeometric-analysis formulation introduced by Hughes, Cottrell and Bazilevs ([CrossedHughes2005]). Bézier extraction is the element-local representation used by the native extracted-space assembly ([CrossedBorden2011]). For nonmatching patch interfaces, established isogeometric mortar methods formulate and analyze a weak coupling with multiplier spaces; their stability depends on the choice of trace and multiplier spaces ([CrossedBrivadis2015]). The present S2 calculation uses collocation for a fixed partial-interface geometry and does not claim those mortar stability properties.

Analysis on audited trivariate models

The analysis layer consumes a geometric model only after its family audit has accepted it. This is a deliberate boundary: a valid volume representation, positive local Jacobians, complete face incidence, and an analysis-suitable field space are separate claims. A determinant certificate for the geometry does not by itself prove that an elasticity discretization is stable or accurate.

Hughes, Cottrell and Bazilevs introduce the use of CAD spline bases for analysis [Hughes2005].

The central fixed-family entry points are analysis_space::build_analysis_space and its family-specific siblings in src/analysis_space.rs. The bracket path exactly elevates degree-one axes to Q2 Bernstein form and identifies face controls by rational physical coordinates after the audited face orientation has been applied. The general adaptive path is in src/adaptive_space.rs: matching Q2 traces are identified, while nonmatching nested traces become exact rational constraint rows. constraint_system::resolve turns those rows into a lift from free masters. This is strong algebraic conformity for the supported trace patterns; it is not a general multipatch coupling method.

For context, common multipatch alternatives include mortar constraints and Nitsche coupling for nonmatching interfaces [Brivadis2015] [Nguyen2014]. These methods impose continuity weakly and introduce their own multiplier-space or stabilization choices. The present supported Q2 adaptive path instead builds exact trace relations for certified whole-face and nested dyadic interfaces. Do not infer support for arbitrary partial, curved, or non-nested overlaps from that implementation. Variable-size extracted elements are implemented in extracted_space and exercised by cargo test --test extracted_space. On the recovered NURBS strip the local THB job uses this assembly route. The separate crossed-tool bending study has a ten-chart geometry with partial interfaces. See Spline fields, extraction, and local refinement for the supported field transfers. On general multipatch inputs, partial footprints need interface-specific treatment, and a THB basis does not automatically resolve those joins.

The small-strain isotropic elasticity implementation is in src/elasticity.rs. assemble uses the supplied tensor-product Gauss order for volume integration; constrained_elasticity::solve_constrained and solve_preconditioned solve the reduced system after boundary and trace constraints have been resolved. Report assembly quadrature, reduced residual, DOFs, solver iterations, and physical error norms separately. A small algebraic residual is evidence about the linear solve, not a discretization-error bound.

The source-level APIs are exercised by Rust tests and by independent reassembly scripts. From the repository root, useful commands are:

cargo test --locked --offline --test elasticity
cargo test --locked --offline --test adaptive_partition
cargo test --locked --offline --test nested_trace
make check-local-thb

make check-local-thb rebuilds the native CLI, runs the THB-specific tests and independent function/traction checks. For the broader project gate use make check; it is substantially more expensive.

Foundational and coupling references

[Hughes2005]

T. J. R. Hughes, J. A. Cottrell, and Y. Bazilevs, “Isogeometric analysis: CAD, finite elements, NURBS, exact geometry and mesh refinement,” Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering, 194 (2005), 4135–4195. DOI 10.1016/j.cma.2004.10.008.

[Brivadis2015]

E. Brivadis, A. Buffa, B. Wohlmuth, and L. Wunderlich, “Isogeometric mortar methods,” Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering, 284 (2015), 292–319. DOI 10.1016/j.cma.2014.09.012. This describes a weak interface-coupling family, not the exact constraint implementation here.

[Nguyen2014]

V. P. Nguyen, P. Kerfriden, M. Brino, S. P. A. Bordas, and E. Bonisoli, “Nitsche’s method for two and three dimensional NURBS patch coupling,” Computational Mechanics, 53(6) (2014), 1163–1182. DOI 10.1007/s00466-013-0955-3.

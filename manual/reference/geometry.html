Geometry representation and certificates

Rational tensor-product maps

The native crate represents a volume block as a rational tensor-product Bézier/NURBS map over the closed unit cube. Control points are homogeneous; positive weights are required where the family contract says so. Physical coordinates and dimensions are in millimeters. Parametric coordinates are dimensionless. The public primitive API starts with RationalBezierMap::new and evaluate in the Bézier map implementation; the crate documentation is the authoritative type-level API.

A model contains both geometry and topology. Geometry includes degrees, knots, homogeneous controls and span domains. Topology includes block sides, oriented face incidences, boundary status, provenance, and persistent identities where the family provides them. Geometry equality alone does not define an interface: restrictions must match under the recorded parameter orientation and, for nested traces, under a certified refinement relation.

NURBS span extraction and exact simplification

The curve utilities in the NURBS curve module extract active Bézier spans by exact knot insertion in integer/rational arithmetic. The source degree and knot multiplicities are preserved as provenance. A common integer scale represents rational knots; homogeneous controls may be rescaled by a common nonzero factor without changing the represented curve. Degree elevation and span subdivision are exact coefficient transformations when they remain within checked arithmetic.

The crate also contains knot-reduction utilities. This module removes only interior degree-one knots, and only when reinsertion reconstructs every homogeneous control component exactly; nonremovable knots are retained. It does not implement tolerance-based approximate knot removal. That is a separate operation with a declared error measure and tolerance, as in the knot-removal literature. Sampling alone does not establish exactness. Unsupported degree, malformed/non-monotone knot vectors, nonpositive weights where disallowed, inconsistent gauges, and integer overflow are rejection cases. NURBS theory and standard extraction operations are covered in Piegl and Tiller, The NURBS Book, 2nd ed., Springer, DOI <https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-59223-2>. This reference describes the general representation; it does not certify any particular SolidCraft model.

For a concrete bounded family, see tests/references/nurbs_strip.recipe.json and the NURBS strip reference. The strip constructor requires compatible monotone boundaries; it does not claim general reparameterization of arbitrary NURBS profiles. For crossed profiles, see tests/references/nurbs_cross_oval.recipe.json and tests/references/nurbs_cross_bores.recipe.json. The latter’s exact event contract is intentionally narrower than general algebraic surface intersection.

Certificates and their domains

SolidCraft separates the following claims:

  • Representation fidelity: the map equals its declared source geometry, or satisfies an explicitly bounded approximation contract.

  • Local regularity: the denominator and Jacobian determinant stay strictly positive on a stated closed parametric domain.

  • Injectivity: the map is one-to-one on that domain. A positive Jacobian alone is local and does not establish global injectivity.

  • Trace compatibility: adjacent maps induce the same oriented geometry on their shared face, possibly after a supported exact subdivision.

  • Coverage: the union of the active block images equals the target material, with disjoint interiors and the required boundary.

  • Analysis suitability: the resulting discrete space is conforming and meets the stated integration or mechanics checks. This is not implied by geometric validity.

Every certificate must name its method, domain, assumptions, bounds, and status. Bernstein-form interval bounds can prove polynomial positivity when all coefficients on a subdomain are strictly positive. Subdivision may make the bound decisive; if a fixed depth or arithmetic budget ends first, the result is indeterminate rather than a proof. The underlying positivity methods are established in the literature. Powers and Reznick bound the degree needed in Pólya-type representations for polynomials positive on polyhedra. Boudaoud, Caruso, and Roy study certificates in the Bernstein basis, including degree elevation and subdivision as ways to obtain decisive coefficients. These are related mathematical results, not specifications of the exact interval tests implemented here. In particular, a positivity result for a polynomial on a simplex or polyhedron does not by itself establish the project’s rational-map certificate. SolidCraft’s certificates are the code and recorded audit outputs for each family. See V. Powers and B. Reznick, “A new bound for Pólya’s theorem with applications to polynomials positive on polyhedra,” Journal of Pure and Applied Algebra 164(1–2) (2001), 221–229, DOI 10.1016/S0022-4049(00)00155-9, and F. Boudaoud, F. Caruso, and M.-F. Roy, “Certificates of Positivity in the Bernstein Basis,” Discrete & Computational Geometry 39(4) (2008), 639–655, DOI 10.1007/s00454-007-9042-x.

The spatial certificate implementation, planar checks, annular audit, polygon-prism audit, and curved-sketch audit implement these checks. A positive sampled determinant is diagnostic only. tests/test_planar_certificate.py and tests/test_annular_certificate.py provide independent reference checks; the Rust tests exercise production APIs.

Exact polygonal input contract

sketch_layer_partition_v1 uses integer millimeter vertices and straight segments. It accepts simple contours subject to the stock, count, and cut limits described in the project README. It does not treat a polygon as a sampled curve. Curved contours use their own families, with explicit degrees, knots, homogeneous controls, and parameter conventions. The polygonal source recipe and selected model preserve loop and feature identifiers; the audit does not silently repair invalid contours.

Local validity is not global coverage

For a block map F, a strict lower bound on det(DF) over the closed cube establishes orientation-preserving local regularity. It does not rule out global self-overlap. Likewise, matching every declared interface does not prove that blocks cover the intended solid. Coverage checks compare exact section areas or family-specific analytic measures and incidence structure; independent point classification and STEP readback are supplemental oracles. An oracle’s positive sample set is evidence for those samples, not a universal proof.

Analysis export

export-analysis creates a versioned conforming space package for the supported geometry family and requested quadrature order. Geometry degree, trace orientation, exact degree elevation, common DOF numbering, and volume quadrature are recorded according to the package schema. Analysis suitability is then checked independently through affine-field reproduction, interface rank, equilibrium residual, and mechanics benchmarks where provided. Geometry certificates do not automatically transfer to a different analysis basis or quadrature rule. See the NURBS space compiler, tensor assembly, analysis space, and solver package.

Bernstein bounds and rational maps

Johnen, Remacle and Geuzaine give a method for bounding Jacobian determinants of curvilinear polynomial finite elements with Bernstein coefficients; their strictly positive lower bound certifies geometric validity for the polynomial elements in their scope. This is a polynomial-element precedent, not a proof of SolidCraft’s rational-map implementation. For a rational SolidCraft chart, the homogeneous determinant numerator and the weight denominator are checked under the family’s stated positive-weight assumptions; the denominator must be accounted for before inferring the physical determinant sign. A failed strict-positivity proof at the subdivision budget is recorded separately from a demonstrated inversion. A boundary-singular fillet must also be identified separately from a regular chart. Johnen et al. (2013), Journal of Computational Physics.

For knot removal, Tiller presents algorithms with error analysis for rational B-spline curves and surfaces; Eck and Hadenfeld study removal of one interior knot and optimize the remaining degrees of freedom under several error norms, including a continuous local min-max criterion. These are references for tolerance-based removal methods, distinct from the crate’s exact degree-one removal operation described above: W. Tiller, “Knot-removal algorithms for NURBS curves and surfaces,” Computer-Aided Design 24(8) (1992), 445–453, DOI 10.1016/0010-4485(92)90012-Y; M. Eck and J. Hadenfeld, “Knot removal for B-spline curves,” Computer Aided Geometric Design 12(3) (1995), 259–282, DOI 10.1016/0167-8396(94)00012-H.
